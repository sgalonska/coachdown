# Why Coachdown exists

> Full specification: https://github.com/sgalonska/coachdown/blob/main/SPEC.md

## A coach does four things at once

During a session, a coach listens closely, asks the next question, takes notes and draws conclusions — all at the same time. Every one of these needs full attention, and the person in front of them deserves it. The notes suffer first: they are written fast, half in the coach's words and half in the coachee's, and the structure exists only in the coach's head.

That structure is needed afterwards. Who said this — the coachee or I? Was this a decision or just an idea? What did we agree to try, and what came of the last experiment? Answering these questions means rereading, sorting and rewriting after every session: summaries, follow-ups, reports to the organisation that pays for the coaching, preparation for the next session. This after-work is often as long as the session itself.

## Lowering the load

Coachdown was created to take load off the coach — during the session and after it.

- **During the session**, the coach keeps writing the way they always do. A handful of one- or two-character markers — `J:` for the coach's own words, `!` for an insight, `=` for a decision, `- [ ]` for an experiment — capture exactly the distinctions that would otherwise be lost. Everything else stays unmarked: by default a line is the coachee's, a question is the coach's.
- **After the session**, the notes are already structured enough that a tool or an AI agent can do the sorting: draft the summary, list the decisions and experiments, prepare the review for next time, fill in the time sheet and the sponsor report from the session metadata.

The goal is not more documentation. It is the same notes, written with a little more intent, so that the after-work largely disappears.

## Making sessions measurable

The second motivation is evaluation. Coaching is meant to create growth, but growth is hard to see from inside a single session — and impossible to compare across sessions when every log is written differently.

With a shared format, sessions can be evaluated automatically:

- **For the coachee:** how their insights, ownership and perspective change over time — who draws the conclusions, which themes fade, which patterns they now name themselves. Completed action points are deliberately not the measure.
- **For the coach:** how they coach — how often they ask instead of advise, how much room the coachee has, which kinds of interventions lead to insights.
- **For research:** anonymised, structural analysis of many sessions, so that the field can learn what actually works.

Every coaching session is a retrospective, and every retrospective helps a system improve. Coachdown makes these retrospectives readable — for people and for machines.

## Notes that belong to both

A coaching log is not the coach's private file. In Coachdown it is a shared document that coach and coachee both write into — the coachee prepares the agenda, notes what happened since the last session, and reviews their own experiments.

- **Ownership:** the log is plain text. It does not depend on any tool, and the coachee can take it with them at the end of the engagement — or bring it to their next coach.
- **Transparency:** the coachee sees everything that is written about them and can add to it or correct it. There are no hidden notes. The log becomes a joint record of the work, and that builds trust.
- **Privacy by design:** structure and content are separate. Who said something, whether it was a question, a decision or an insight — all of this can be evaluated without reading a single word of what was said. Coachdown is designed so that sessions can be analysed without anyone reading them.

## Further benefits

- **Continuity.** A co-coach, a supervisor or a new coach can understand the engagement without having been there — and so can the coach, years later, when a former coachee returns.
- **One format instead of many.** More and more tools, including AI assistants, support coaches. Without a shared format, each invents its own, and notes get stuck in one of them. Coachdown keeps editors, analysis tools and research compatible.
- **A light scaffold.** The standard sections — Since last time, Review, Discussion, Decisions, Experiments — give a session a structure without prescribing it. This helps coaches who are new to the profession as much as experienced ones on a busy day.
- **Professional development.** Coaches often need to document their practice for certification or supervision. Session metadata such as dates, duration and type provides this without exposing any content.

## Where it came from

Coachdown grew out of years of coaching experience — out of the practical question of how a coach can stay fully present in a session and still end up with notes that are useful afterwards. It formalises habits many coaches already have, such as one rolling document per coachee with the newest session on top, and adds only what is needed to read such notes reliably later.
